# Original synthetic writing examples

These short illustrations demonstrate the revised workflow. They are not completed studies, external-source evidence or transcripts of the behavioral evaluation.

## Proposal without observed data

**Brief:** A museum has 30 existing label texts and two weeks for a proposal. Visitor data and permission to alter displays are unavailable. Its initial interest is whether labels improve recall.

**Feasible proposal:** Examine how the accessible labels present and repeat key ideas, producing a documented coding scheme and a conditional visitor-study protocol. Textual patterns can support a description of these labels; they cannot establish effects on visitors' recall.

| Question | Proposed contribution | Required evidence/access | Method | Planned analysis/argument | Feasible deliverable and limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| How do these labels introduce and reinforce key ideas? | A transparent account of the accessible label set that can inform a later visitor-study design | The 30 complete texts and their available context; no visitor measurements | Define and pilot a coding scheme, retain excerpts and ambiguous cases, then inspect all 30 texts | Compare recurring patterns and counterexamples; distinguish descriptive observations from interpretation | Coding scheme, passage-linked account and conditional study protocol within two weeks; no claim about recall effects |

The proposed contribution weakens if the labels provide too little context for reproducible distinctions, or if the chosen categories merely restate their wording without a useful comparison. A simpler descriptive inventory is an alternative. Visitor recall remains a separate question requiring suitable measurements, access, permissions and a justified design. A prospective schedule can allocate the first week to piloting/revision and the second to the full account and protocol; it does not imply that a visitor study has occurred.

## Manuscript excerpt for a completed synthetic null study

**Provided result R1:** Two interfaces were compared on the same task, with 40 participants per arm. The prespecified outcome was seconds saved by interface B. The locked analysis reports an estimate of 0.0 seconds and a 95% confidence interval from −1.5 to 1.5 seconds. No equivalence margin was specified.

**Results:** The estimated time saved by B was 0.0 seconds (95% CI −1.5 to 1.5; 40 participants per arm).

**Interpretation:** This analysis did not detect a difference in completion time. The confidence interval spans 1.5 seconds of disadvantage to 1.5 seconds of benefit for B. Practical importance requires task-specific context. The result does not establish equivalence or prove that the interfaces have identical effects. Interpretation is limited to the task and conditions actually studied; details absent from this synthetic brief must not be invented in a full manuscript.

| Claim/result ID | Authoritative artifact and locator | Abstract/text/display occurrences | Agreement, correction and status |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Supplied locked analysis summary above | Abstract estimate, Results sentence, any results table and caption | Retain 0.0 seconds, CI −1.5 to 1.5 and n=40 per arm everywhere; remove any claim of demonstrated benefit or equivalence |

For a separate consistency example, a locked completion-time table with A=10 seconds and B=12 seconds cannot support an abstract saying B was two seconds faster. With lower time defined as better, B was two seconds slower. Correct affected prose and captions; preserve the authoritative table.
